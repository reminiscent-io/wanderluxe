# Product

## Register

product

## Users

The **group organizer** — the person in a couple, family, or friend group
who takes on planning. They're the one collecting flight confirmations
in their inbox, juggling everyone's preferences, and trying to keep a
shared trip from devolving into a thread of "wait, where are we staying
again?" They skew younger (late 20s to 40s), are reasonably tech-fluent,
and care about taste — they want to plan something the group will be
impressed by, not just functional.

They use WanderLuxe across two contexts: focused planning sessions at
home (laptop, browser tab open while researching), and quick on-the-go
edits from phones (forwarding a confirmation, checking tomorrow's
itinerary, adjusting a reservation). Real-time collaboration with
co-travelers is the killer feature, not a side note.

## Product Purpose

WanderLuxe collapses the coordination tax of group trip planning into a
single, beautiful, shared picture. Every booking, reservation, and
activity finds its place on a timeline that the whole group can see and
contribute to. AI does the grunt work — paste a confirmation, get a
timeline event; ask the assistant for a restaurant, watch it appear on
the right day. The organizer ends up with something they're proud to
hand to the group, and the group ends up with something they actually
look forward to opening.

Success: the organizer feels like the trip is theirs, the group treats
the itinerary as the trip's home base, and the planning effort feels
like a fraction of what it would have taken in spreadsheets and threads.

## Brand Personality

Warm, editorial, intentional.

The voice is closer to a Condé Nast Traveler feature than a SaaS
release note. Confident without shouting. Generous with whitespace,
photography, and typography. Quietly opinionated — like Aesop's product
pages, the restraint *is* the luxury signal. Never breathy or
saccharine; never transactional or banner-y.

## Anti-references

- **Booking.com / Expedia / Kayak / TripAdvisor** — transactional clutter,
  banner-heavy, search-result-list mentality, every pixel screams price
  and urgency. WanderLuxe is the opposite of this energy.
- **Generic SaaS dashboards** — cold blue palettes, identical
  icon-plus-heading card grids, hero-metric templates, lifeless
  enterprise UI with no cultural texture or point of view.
- **Loud "luxury" cliches** — gold-on-black, marble textures, "Premium"
  badges, champagne emojis, yacht-marketing aesthetics. Real luxury
  whispers.

## Design Principles

1. **Do work for the user, don't ask for it.** Manual data entry is a
   tax. The AI assistant, document parsing, automatic day generation,
   and chat-driven timeline edits are not features — they're the
   operating principle. If a screen demands typing where pasting,
   uploading, or chatting could do the job, the screen is wrong.

2. **Make their taste look good.** The organizer is curating an
   experience for people they care about. The interface is their
   gallery wall, not a CMS. Photography is treated generously, choices
   are framed with confidence, and the organizer's plan looks
   considered — even when it's a rough draft.

3. **One clear picture.** Group trips are chaos by default: multiple
   travelers, scattered confirmations, conflicting calendars. The app's
   job is to dissolve that chaos into a single, scannable, real-time
   timeline that the whole group trusts. Hierarchy, not density.

4. **Editorial, not transactional.** Compose like a magazine spread:
   purposeful whitespace, photographic anchors, typographic hierarchy
   doing the heavy lifting. Prices, statuses, and booking-noise stay
   subordinate to story and place. We are not a search results page.

5. **Quiet confidence.** Aesop, not Las Vegas. The warm palette,
   editorial type, and restrained motion *are* the brand — we don't
   need badges, sparkles, or "luxe" labels to prove it. If a design
   choice would feel out of place in a Cereal magazine layout, it's
   the wrong choice.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- **WCAG 2.1 AA** as the baseline target. Color contrast, focus states,
  keyboard navigation, semantic structure, screen-reader labels.
- **Effort minimization as inclusion.** Users skew younger and
  tech-fluent, but the whole product philosophy is built around
  reducing effort — paste-and-parse over forms, chat-to-edit over
  modals. This benefits anyone with limited time, attention, or
  patience for data entry, which is everyone.
- **Reduced motion** respected via `prefers-reduced-motion`; animations
  remain meaningful, never load-bearing for comprehension.
- **Mobile-first responsiveness** — itinerary glanceability and
  thumb-reach matter; the app is used in transit as often as at a desk.
